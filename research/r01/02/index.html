01 · RESEARCH PURPOSE

Why Act 597 matters

The Human Rights Commission of Malaysia Act 1999 provides the domestic statutory foundation for SUHAKAM. For this research project, the Act is examined not merely as legislation establishing an institution, but as part of the legal architecture through which human rights standards are studied, promoted and connected with Malaysia's domestic legal framework.

01

Statutory Foundation

Act 597 establishes the legislative basis for the existence and operation of the Human Rights Commission of Malaysia.

02

Institutional Mandate

The legislation provides the legal framework within which SUHAKAM carries out its human rights functions.

03

Domestic Legal Position

The Act forms part of Malaysian domestic law and must therefore be analysed within the country's constitutional and statutory framework.

04

International Interface

The Commission's statutory role provides an important point of institutional analysis when examining Malaysia's engagement with international human rights standards.

03 · INSTITUTIONAL POSITION

SUHAKAM as a legal bridge

The Commission occupies an important analytical position between Malaysia's domestic legal system and the wider international human rights framework. This does not mean that international treaties automatically become domestic law. Rather, it creates an institutional space in which international standards can be studied, promoted and considered alongside Malaysian law.

Institutional Analysis

Domestic law and international standards can interact without being legally identical.

This distinction is central to the research. The statutory role of SUHAKAM should be examined separately from the domestic legal effect of international treaties. The Commission may engage with international human rights standards while Malaysian courts and legislation remain governed by the domestic constitutional and statutory framework.

04 · LEGAL ANALYSIS

Questions the statute raises

The Act is more than an institutional label.

A legal research approach requires examination of how the statutory framework positions the Commission within Malaysia's broader constitutional, legislative and international human rights environment.

01

What is the domestic legal foundation?

Act 597 provides the statutory foundation for the establishment and operation of SUHAKAM.

02

What is the institutional role?

The Commission's statutory functions should be examined to understand how Parliament has defined its role in Malaysia's human rights architecture.

03

How does international law enter the analysis?

International human rights instruments may provide standards and reference points, but their domestic legal effect must be analysed separately from the existence of the Commission itself.

04

Where does SUHAKAM fit?

The Commission can be studied as an institutional intermediary connecting domestic human rights practice, public institutions and international human rights mechanisms.

05 · LEGAL DISTINCTION

Two different legal questions

One of the most important safeguards in this research is to avoid treating institutional engagement with international standards as equivalent to incorporation of international treaties into domestic law.

Institutional Engagement

  • Human rights promotion
  • Public education
  • Institutional recommendations
  • Engagement with international standards
  • Human rights monitoring and research

Domestic Legal Effect

  • Constitutional provisions
  • Acts of Parliament
  • Judicial interpretation
  • Domestic incorporation of treaties
  • Applicable Malaysian law
06 · PRELIMINARY FINDING

Research position

Preliminary Finding

Act 597 is best understood as the domestic statutory foundation of Malaysia's national human rights institution. Its significance within this research lies in the institutional relationship it creates between statutory human rights work in Malaysia and the wider development, interpretation and promotion of international human rights standards.
