01 · RESEARCH PURPOSE

Why study the ICCPR in relation to Malaysia?

The ICCPR is one of the principal international human rights treaties concerning civil and political rights. Its inclusion in this research allows a distinction to be drawn between international human rights standards and Malaysia's formal treaty participation.

Central Research Question

What is the significance of the ICCPR for analysing civil and political rights in Malaysia when Malaysia is not a State Party to the Covenant?

02 · RIGHTS ARCHITECTURE

The civil and political rights framework

The Covenant contains a broad catalogue of rights and freedoms. For research purposes, these can be organised into several analytical groups.

01

Life & Security

The Covenant protects rights concerning life, liberty and security of the person.

02

Fair Process

The framework contains protections relating to arrest, detention, trial and procedural justice.

03

Expression

The Covenant addresses freedom of expression and related civil liberties.

04

Religion & Belief

Freedom of thought, conscience and religion forms part of the Covenant's rights framework.

05

Political Participation

The Covenant includes provisions concerning participation in public affairs and political life.

06

Equality

Equality and non-discrimination are important components of the Covenant's civil and political rights framework.

04 · MALAYSIA
Treaty Status

Malaysia is not a State Party to the ICCPR.

This status is important because it prevents the research from presenting ICCPR provisions as treaty obligations binding on Malaysia in the same manner as they would bind a State Party.

At the same time, the Covenant remains relevant to comparative and international human rights analysis, particularly when examining international standards concerning civil and political rights.

Not a State Party does not mean irrelevant.

The research must distinguish between the legal consequences of treaty participation and the broader analytical relevance of international human rights standards.

Any claim that a particular ICCPR provision reflects customary international law requires separate analysis of the relevant rule, State practice and opinio juris. It should not be assumed solely from the existence of the Covenant.

06 · ANALYTICAL QUESTIONS

Questions guiding further research

QUESTION 01

How does Malaysia's non-party status affect the analysis?

The research should distinguish treaty obligations from broader international human rights standards.

QUESTION 02

How have Malaysian courts treated ICCPR norms?

Relevant judicial decisions should be examined individually rather than assuming a uniform judicial approach.

QUESTION 03

Which ICCPR norms may have wider international relevance?

Any customary international law argument requires independent legal analysis of the specific norm concerned.

QUESTION 04

What would treaty accession change?

A future policy or legal study could examine the international and domestic implications of Malaysia becoming a State Party.

07 · PRELIMINARY FINDING

The ICCPR is a comparative international standard, not a Malaysian treaty obligation.

Because Malaysia is not a State Party to the ICCPR, the research must not describe Covenant provisions as directly binding treaty obligations of Malaysia. Its value within this project lies in examining international standards, comparative human rights law and the relationship between international norms and Malaysia's domestic framework.

Research Integrity

This page does not treat the ICCPR as binding treaty law applicable to Malaysia. It also does not assume that ICCPR provisions automatically constitute customary international law. Each proposition must be supported by appropriate primary authority and the specific legal question being examined.
