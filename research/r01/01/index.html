Research Purpose

The Federal Constitution is the principal domestic legal framework for examining the protection of fundamental liberties in Malaysia.

Within R01, the Constitution provides the domestic baseline against which legislation, judicial decisions, institutional practice and international human rights standards can be examined.

The research therefore treats constitutional law and international human rights law as related but legally distinct fields of analysis.

Central Research Question

How does international human rights law interact with the Malaysian domestic legal system, particularly where constitutional rights, legislation, judicial interpretation and international obligations intersect?

Fundamental Liberties

Article 5

Life & Personal Liberty

Protection concerning life and personal liberty, subject to the constitutional and legal framework governing deprivation of liberty.

Article 6

Slavery & Forced Labour

Constitutional protection against slavery and forced labour, subject to the provisions expressly recognised by the Constitution.

Article 7

Criminal Justice Safeguards

Protection concerning retrospective criminal laws and repeated trials.

Article 8

Equality

Equality before the law and equal protection of the law within the constitutional framework.

Article 9

Movement

Constitutional provisions concerning freedom of movement and its permissible restrictions.

Articles 10–13

Other Liberties

Specified freedoms, religious liberty, educational rights and property rights within the constitutional framework.

05

Domestic Constitution & International Law

Domestic Framework

Constitutional Rights

Rights recognised and protected under Malaysian constitutional law, together with the legislation and judicial decisions that operate within that framework.

International Framework

Human Rights Standards

International treaties, declarations, treaty-body materials and other international standards relevant to the protection of human rights.

The distinction matters because Malaysia's participation in an international treaty and the treaty's domestic legal effect are separate legal questions.

International obligations should therefore not automatically be described as directly enforceable domestic law without examining the relevant constitutional, statutory and judicial framework.

Selected Jurisprudence

2009 · FEDERAL COURT

Lee Kwan Woh v Public Prosecutor

Relevant to the interpretation of constitutional protections, particularly Article 5 and Article 8, and the judicial approach to fundamental liberties.

2010 · FEDERAL COURT

Sivarasa Rasiah v Badan Peguam Malaysia & Anor

Relevant to constitutional rights analysis and the judicial treatment of restrictions on fundamental liberties.

2019 · FEDERAL COURT

Alma Nudo Atenza v Public Prosecutor

Relevant to constitutional interpretation and the scope of fundamental liberties within Malaysian law.

Preliminary Research Finding

The Federal Constitution provides the principal domestic framework through which fundamental liberties are legally protected in Malaysia. International human rights standards may provide an important comparative and interpretive reference, but their precise domestic legal effect must be analysed separately from the constitutional rights themselves.
