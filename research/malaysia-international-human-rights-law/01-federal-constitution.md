# WHY THIS SOURCE MATTERS

The Federal Constitution is a central primary legal source for examining the domestic protection of fundamental liberties in Malaysia.

**Part II — Fundamental Liberties** contains Articles 5 to 13, covering matters including personal liberty, equality, freedom of movement, freedom of speech, assembly and association, freedom of religion, education and property.

For this research project, the Constitution provides the domestic legal framework against which relevant international human rights standards can be examined.

# INITIAL LEGAL OBSERVATIONS

The constitutional provisions should not be treated as isolated statements of rights.

The research will examine:

1. The wording of each relevant constitutional provision.
2. Constitutional qualifications and restrictions attached to particular rights.
3. How Malaysian courts have interpreted the provisions.
4. How domestic constitutional protections can be compared with relevant international human rights standards.
5. The legal significance of Malaysia's participation in particular international human rights treaties.

For example, Article 10 recognises freedoms of speech and expression, peaceful assembly and association, while also providing constitutional conditions concerning restrictions imposed by law.
