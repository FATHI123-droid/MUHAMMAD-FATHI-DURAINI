# CRPD Treaty Body Materials: Malaysia

## 01 · PURPOSE

This file records materials relating to Malaysia's engagement with the **Committee on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD Committee)**.

The materials will support research concerning:

* Disability rights
* Equality and non-discrimination
* Accessibility
* Legal capacity
* Inclusive education
* Health
* Employment
* Social protection
* Participation in public life
* Human rights and development
* Treaty implementation in Malaysia

---

# 02 · TREATY FRAMEWORK

**Treaty:** Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD)

**Treaty body:** Committee on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities

**Malaysia:** State Party

The CRPD provides an international human rights framework concerning the rights and participation of persons with disabilities.

The research will examine how the international standards contained in the CRPD relate to Malaysian domestic law, institutions and public policy.

---

### Article 9

Accessibility.

### Article 12

### Article 13

### Article 19

Living independently and being included in the community.

### Article 21

Freedom of expression and access to information.

### Article 24

Education.

### Article 25

Health.

### Article 27

Work and employment.

### Article 28

Adequate standard of living and social protection.

### Article 29

Participation in political and public life.

### Article 33

National implementation and monitoring.

These provisions provide a useful framework for examining the relationship between international disability rights standards and Malaysian law.

# 05 · NATIONAL IMPLEMENTATION

Article 33 of the CRPD is particularly relevant to institutional analysis.

It concerns:

* National implementation
* Government coordination
* Independent monitoring
* Participation of persons with disabilities and representative organisations

This provides a useful framework for examining Malaysian institutions involved in disability rights.

Potential institutions for further research include:

* Relevant Federal Government ministries
* SUHAKAM
* Government agencies responsible for persons with disabilities
* Courts and judicial institutions
* Civil society organisations
* Organisations representing persons with disabilities

# 06 · MALAYSIAN DOMESTIC LAW

The CRPD should be examined alongside relevant Malaysian legal and policy frameworks.

Potential areas include:

* Persons with Disabilities Act 2008
* Federal Constitution
* Education legislation
* Employment legislation
* Social protection frameworks
* Accessibility standards
* Public health policies
* Social development policies

The research should determine the precise relationship between each domestic framework and the corresponding CRPD standards.

It should not assume that a domestic law implements a CRPD provision merely because both concern the same subject.

# 07 · HUMAN RIGHTS AND DEVELOPMENT

The CRPD is particularly relevant to the **Human Rights & Development** component of this portfolio.

Disability rights can be examined through the relationship between:

**Non-discrimination → Accessibility → Participation → Education → Employment → Social Protection → Inclusive Development**

This allows the research to move beyond a purely legal analysis and consider how human rights standards interact with development policy.

# 08 · SUHAKAM

SUHAKAM is relevant to CRPD research because its statutory mandate includes the promotion and protection of human rights in Malaysia.

Further research should examine:

* SUHAKAM reports concerning persons with disabilities
* SUHAKAM recommendations
* SUHAKAM submissions to UN mechanisms
* Institutional monitoring
* Disability rights complaints
* Accessibility and equality issues


This framework can later be used to develop a focused policy brief.

---

# 12 · RESEARCH CAUTION

The research must distinguish between:

* CRPD treaty obligations
* Malaysian domestic legislation
* Government policy
* Treaty-body recommendations
* SUHAKAM recommendations
* Civil society claims
* Academic interpretations
* The researcher's own analysis

A treaty-body recommendation should not automatically be described as a binding domestic legal requirement.

Similarly, the existence of domestic legislation should not automatically be treated as proof of complete implementation of a treaty obligation.
