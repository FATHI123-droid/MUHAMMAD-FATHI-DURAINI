# Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities

### International Human Rights Instrument 03

**Research Project:** Malaysia and International Human Rights Law

---

## SOURCE IDENTIFICATION

**Title:** Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities
**Abbreviation:** CRPD
**Adopted:** 13 December 2006
**Entry into Force:** 3 May 2008
**Source Type:** International Human Rights Treaty
**Depositary:** Secretary-General of the United Nations

Malaysia signed the Convention on **8 April 2008** and acceded to it on **19 July 2010**. The Convention entered into force for Malaysia on **18 August 2010**.

---

# WHY THIS SOURCE MATTERS

The CRPD provides the international human rights framework concerning the rights and participation of persons with disabilities.

For this research project, the Convention provides a useful case study for examining the relationship between:

* International human rights obligations
* Malaysian legislation
* Constitutional protections
* Public policy
* Institutional responsibilities
* Human rights implementation

The CRPD is particularly relevant to the **human rights and development** dimension of this research because it connects equality and non-discrimination with accessibility, participation, inclusion and independent living.

---

# KEY PRINCIPLES

The Convention is based on principles including:

* Respect for inherent dignity and individual autonomy
* Non-discrimination
* Full and effective participation and inclusion
* Respect for difference
* Equality of opportunity
* Accessibility
* Equality between men and women
* Respect for the evolving capacities of children with disabilities

These principles provide a framework for analysing how disability rights are addressed through Malaysian law and policy.

# ARTICLE 5 — EQUALITY AND NON-DISCRIMINATION

Article 5 recognises equality before and under the law and requires States Parties to prohibit discrimination on the basis of disability.

This provision provides a useful starting point for comparing:

**CRPD standards**

with

**Malaysian constitutional and statutory protections.**

The research should examine how disability-related equality is addressed within the Malaysian legal framework.

---

# ARTICLE 9 — ACCESSIBILITY

Article 9 addresses accessibility.

It covers access to:

* The physical environment
* Transportation
* Information
* Communications
* Other facilities and services open or provided to the public

For the Malaysian context, this creates potential research areas involving:

* Public infrastructure
* Digital accessibility
* Public transportation
* Government services
* Education
* Employment
* Access to information

Evidence should be drawn from legislation, official policies, institutional reports and empirical research.

---

# ARTICLE 12 — EQUAL RECOGNITION BEFORE THE LAW

Article 12 concerns the equal recognition of persons with disabilities before the law.

This provision is relevant to research concerning:

* Legal capacity
* Decision-making
* Guardianship
* Access to legal processes
* Protection of rights

The research should distinguish the CRPD's international standard from the specific provisions and judicial interpretation of Malaysian domestic law.

---

# ARTICLE 13 — ACCESS TO JUSTICE

Article 13 requires effective access to justice for persons with disabilities on an equal basis with others.

Potential research areas include:

* Court accessibility
* Legal assistance
* Procedural accommodation
* Communication support
* Access to legal information
* Participation in judicial proceedings

This provision is particularly relevant to a research portfolio focused on **human rights and law**.

---

# ARTICLE 24 — EDUCATION

# ARTICLE 27 — WORK AND EMPLOYMENT

# ARTICLE 29 — POLITICAL AND PUBLIC LIFE

Article 29 addresses participation in political and public life.

This includes the opportunity for persons with disabilities to participate in political and public affairs on an equal basis with others.

Potential research areas include:

* Voting accessibility
* Political participation
* Public consultation
* Representation

* Participation in public decision-making

---

# ARTICLE 33 — NATIONAL IMPLEMENTATION AND MONITORING

Article 33 concerns national implementation and monitoring mechanisms.

States Parties are required to designate one or more focal points within government for matters relating to implementation and to consider the establishment or designation of a coordination mechanism.

The Article also requires States Parties to maintain, strengthen, designate or establish an independent framework to promote, protect and monitor implementation of the Convention, taking into account the principles relating to national human rights institutions.

This provision is particularly relevant to the study of **SUHAKAM and national human rights institutions**.

The research should examine the relationship between:

**CRPD Article 33**

↓

**National Government Mechanisms**

↓

**Independent Monitoring**

↓

**National Human Rights Institutions**

↓

**Implementation in Malaysia**

# MALAYSIA'S TREATY STATUS

Malaysia acceded to the CRPD on **19 July 2010**.

The OHCHR Treaty Body Database records Malaysia as a State Party to the CRPD.

Malaysia's treaty participation should be examined separately from its domestic implementation.

---

# TREATY-BODY MONITORING

The Committee on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities monitors implementation of the Convention by States Parties.

The OHCHR Treaty Body Database records Malaysia's initial State party report as **CRPD/C/MYS/1**, submitted in 2012.

These materials can be used to examine:

* Malaysia's description of implementation
* Questions raised by the Committee
* Government responses
* Concluding observations
* Recommendations
* Follow-up measures

Treaty-body observations should be distinguished from the legally binding text of the Convention itself.
