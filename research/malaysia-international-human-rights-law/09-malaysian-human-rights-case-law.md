# Malaysian Human Rights Case Law

## 01 · PURPOSE

This file records Malaysian judicial decisions relevant to the study of:

* Fundamental liberties
* Constitutional rights
* Human rights
* International law
* International human rights standards
* The relationship between international law and Malaysian domestic law

The cases will be examined as part of the legal analysis for **Research Project 01: Malaysia and International Human Rights Law**.

---

# 02 · RESEARCH APPROACH

Cases will not be included merely because they concern a human rights issue.

Each case should be assessed according to:

| Criterion                | Question                                                                              |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Constitutional relevance | Does the case concern a constitutional right or fundamental liberty?                  |
| Human rights relevance   | Does it concern a recognised human rights issue?                                      |
| International law        | Does the judgment refer to international law or international human rights standards? |
| Legal significance       | Does the decision contribute to understanding Malaysian law?                          |
| Research relevance       | Does it directly assist the research question?                                        |

# 04 · INTERNATIONAL LAW AND MALAYSIAN COURTS

A central issue for later research is how Malaysian courts approach international law and international human rights standards.

The research should distinguish between:

### International treaties binding on Malaysia

Treaties to which Malaysia is a State Party may be relevant to understanding Malaysia's international obligations.

However, the domestic legal effect of a treaty must be determined according to Malaysian constitutional and statutory law.

### International instruments not binding on Malaysia as treaties

Documents such as the Universal Declaration of Human Rights may still have interpretive or normative relevance, but their legal status must not automatically be equated with domestic legislation.

### Foreign and international judicial decisions

Decisions from international courts and foreign courts may provide comparative or persuasive material.

They should not automatically be treated as binding Malaysian law.

# 05 · CONSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

The cases collected in this archive will be examined primarily in relation to **Part II of the Federal Constitution**, including:

* Article 5 — Life and personal liberty
* Article 8 — Equality
* Article 9 — Freedom of movement
* Article 10 — Freedom of speech, assembly and association
* Article 11 — Freedom of religion
* Article 12 — Rights in respect of education
* Article 13 — Rights to property

The purpose is to examine how Malaysian courts interpret and apply these constitutional protections.

# 06 · QUESTIONS FOR CASE ANALYSIS

Each case should eventually be analysed using the following structure:

### 1. Facts

What happened?

### 2. Legal Issue

What legal question did the court have to determine?

### 3. Relevant Law

Which constitutional provisions, statutes or legal principles were involved?

### 4. Judgment

What did the court decide?

### 5. Reasoning

What legal reasoning did the court use?

### 6. International Law

Did the court refer to international treaties, declarations, customary international law or foreign/international decisions?

### 7. Significance

What does the decision contribute to understanding Malaysian human rights law?

### 8. Research Relevance

How does the case contribute to the research question?

---

# 07 · RESEARCH CAUTION

Case summaries should be based on the actual judgment rather than secondary descriptions alone.

The research should distinguish between:

* The court's holding
* Judicial reasoning
* Obiter statements
* Counsel's submissions
* Academic commentary
* Later interpretations of the judgment

International human rights instruments should not be described as binding domestic law unless their legal basis has been established.
