# Human Rights Commission of Malaysia Act 1999

### Primary Legal Source 02

**Research Project:** Malaysia and International Human Rights Law

## SOURCE IDENTIFICATION

**Title:** Human Rights Commission of Malaysia Act 1999
**Act:** Act 597
**Source Type:** Primary Legal Source
**Jurisdiction:** Malaysia
**Institution:** Parliament of Malaysia

The Human Rights Commission of Malaysia Act 1999 establishes the statutory framework for the Human Rights Commission of Malaysia (SUHAKAM). The current Act incorporates amendments that came into force on 10 July 2024.

---

# WHY THIS SOURCE MATTERS

Act 597 is a central primary legal source for understanding the legal mandate, functions and powers of SUHAKAM.

Section 2 defines "human rights" by reference to the fundamental liberties enshrined in Part II of the Federal Constitution. Section 4 sets out the Commission's functions, including human rights education, advising and assisting the Government, recommending consideration of treaties and other international instruments, and inquiring into complaints concerning infringements of human rights.

For this research project, Act 597 is particularly important because it connects Malaysia's domestic institutional framework with the broader study of international human rights standards.

# SECTION 2 — DEFINITION OF HUMAN RIGHTS

Section 2 defines "human rights" as the fundamental liberties enshrined in Part II of the Federal Constitution.

This provision is important for understanding the domestic statutory framework within which SUHAKAM operates.

It also provides a starting point for examining the relationship between:

* Constitutional rights
* Statutory human rights institutions
* International human rights standards
* Malaysia's treaty participation

---

# SECTION 4 — FUNCTIONS AND POWERS

Section 4(1) identifies four principal functions of the Commission:

1. To promote awareness of and provide education in relation to human rights.
2. To advise and assist the Government in formulating legislation and administrative directives and procedures and recommend necessary measures.
3. To recommend to the Government regarding subscription or accession to treaties and other international instruments in the field of human rights.
4. To inquire into complaints regarding infringements of human rights.

These functions demonstrate that SUHAKAM's statutory role extends beyond receiving complaints and includes education, policy advice, legislative engagement and consideration of international human rights instruments.

---

# SECTION 4(2) — RESEARCH AND INSTITUTIONAL ENGAGEMENT

Section 4(2) provides additional powers for the Commission to discharge its functions.

These include powers relating to:

* Human rights research
* Programmes, seminars and workshops
* Dissemination of research
* Advising Government and relevant authorities
* Studying and verifying alleged infringements
* Visiting places of detention
* Making recommendations
* Public statements concerning human rights

This is particularly relevant to understanding the type of evidence-based research and policy-oriented work that can support human rights institutions.

# 2024 AMENDMENTS

The 2024 amendments to Act 597 came into force on 10 July 2024.

SUHAKAM stated that the amendments strengthened the Commission's functions and investigative framework, enhanced the Office of the Children's Commissioner and expanded certain powers relating to visits to places of detention, institutions and other relevant premises.

The amended Act should therefore be used when conducting current legal analysis rather than relying exclusively on older versions of Act 597.
