# Academic Literature

## Malaysia and International Human Rights Law

This file records academic literature relevant to **Research Project 01: Malaysia and International Human Rights Law**.

The purpose is to build a scholarly foundation for analysing the relationship between international human rights standards, Malaysian domestic law, courts and institutions.

---

# 01 · INTERNATIONAL LAW AND DOMESTIC LAW

## M. Shah Alam — Enforcement of International Human Rights Law by Domestic Courts: A Theoretical and Practical Study

**Author:** M. Shah Alam

**Publication:** Netherlands International Law Review, Vol. 53, Issue 3, 2006, pp. 399–438

**Research relevance:**

Examines the relationship between international human rights law and domestic courts, including the implications of dualism, monism and implementing legislation.

The article is useful for developing the theoretical framework concerning the domestic enforcement of international human rights norms.

**Key themes:**

* International human rights law
* Domestic courts
* Dualism
* Monism
* Implementing legislation
* Treaty enforcement

**Research use:**

This literature will support the theoretical distinction between international legal obligations and domestic legal enforceability.

---

# 02 · INTERNATIONAL TREATIES AND DOMESTIC INTERPRETATION

## Teresa A. Doherty — The Application of Human Rights Treaties in the Development of Domestic and International Law: A Personal Perspective

**Author:** Teresa A. Doherty

**Publication:** Leiden Journal of International Law, 2009

**Research relevance:**

Examines the role of international human rights treaties in domestic legal interpretation, particularly in common law jurisdictions.

The article discusses the possibility of domestic courts referring to human rights treaties when interpreting domestic law even where the treaties have not been incorporated into domestic legislation.

**Key themes:**

* Human rights treaties
* Domestic interpretation
* Common law
* Judicial reasoning
* Treaty incorporation

**Research use:**

This literature is relevant to the research question concerning the possible interpretive influence of international human rights standards in Malaysian courts.

---

# 03 · UN TREATY BODIES AND DOMESTIC COURTS

## Machiko Kanetake — UN Human Rights Treaty Monitoring Bodies Before Domestic Courts

**Author:** Machiko Kanetake

**Publication:** International & Comparative Law Quarterly, Vol. 67, Issue 1, 2018, pp. 201–232

**Research relevance:**

Examines how domestic courts engage with findings, comments, observations and views of UN human rights treaty-monitoring bodies.

The article highlights that judicial engagement with international human rights monitoring bodies can vary significantly between jurisdictions.

**Key themes:**

* Treaty-monitoring bodies
* Domestic courts
* Judicial engagement
* General comments
* State reporting
* International human rights interpretation

**Research use:**

This literature provides a comparative framework for considering how international human rights institutions may interact with domestic judicial reasoning.

# 04 · TREATY-MAKING IN MALAYSIA

## Abdul Ghafur Maung Sein Khin — Treaty-Making Power in Federal States with Special Reference to the Malaysian Position

**Author:** Abdul Ghafur Maung Sein Khin

**Publication:** Journal of Malaysian and Comparative Law, Vol. 30, 2003, pp. 65–88

**Research relevance:**

Examines treaty-making power in federal states with particular attention to Malaysia's constitutional framework, case law and treaty-making practice.

**Key themes:**

* Treaty-making power
* Malaysian constitutional law
* Federalism
* Executive authority
* International agreements
* Malaysian treaty practice

**Research use:**

This source is relevant to understanding the institutional and constitutional dimension of Malaysia's participation in international treaties.

# 05 · SUHAKAM AS A NATIONAL HUMAN RIGHTS INSTITUTION

## Testing the Mettle of National Human Rights Institutions: A Case Study of the Human Rights Commission of Malaysia

**Publication:** Asian Journal of International Law, Vol. 1, Issue 1, 2011, pp. 165–198

**Research relevance:**

Examines the establishment and development of SUHAKAM, its relationship with the Malaysian Government and civil society, and its engagement with international human rights mechanisms.

The study is particularly relevant to understanding the role and institutional limitations of a national human rights institution in Malaysia.

**Key themes:**

* SUHAKAM
* National human rights institutions
* Paris Principles
* Government relations
* Civil society
* International human rights mechanisms
* Human rights treaty ratification

**Research use:**

This literature will support the institutional component of the research, particularly the role of SUHAKAM in promoting human rights standards and engaging with international instruments.

**Research relevance:**

Examines the application of the Geneva Convention framework in Malaysia and the relationship between international humanitarian law and domestic legislation.

**Key themes:**

* Geneva Conventions
* International humanitarian law
* Malaysian legislation
* Domestic implementation
* International law

**Research use:**

Although the present research focuses primarily on international human rights law, this literature provides useful comparative material for understanding how international legal obligations may be implemented through domestic legislation.

# 08 · LITERATURE GAP

The initial literature collection indicates several areas requiring further research.

### Gap 1 — Malaysian Human Rights Treaty Practice

Further research is required on how Malaysia has implemented its obligations under treaties to which it is a State Party.

### Gap 2 — Judicial Treatment

More systematic examination of Malaysian judgments is required to determine when and how international human rights standards are referred to in judicial reasoning.

### Gap 3 — SUHAKAM

Further research is needed on the relationship between SUHAKAM's statutory functions, international human rights standards and domestic policy development.

### Gap 4 — Non-Ratified Treaties

Further research could examine the legal and policy significance of international human rights treaties to which Malaysia is not currently a State Party.

### Gap 5 — Human Rights and Development

The relationship between economic, social and cultural rights and Malaysia's development policies requires further interdisciplinary research.

# 09 · LITERATURE REVIEW METHOD

Future literature will be assessed according to:

1. Author and institutional credibility
2. Publication venue
3. Peer-review or scholarly status

5. Methodological quality
6. Relationship to the research question

Different scholarly interpretations will be identified and evaluated according to their legal and evidential basis.
