# International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR)

## 01 · BASIC INFORMATION

**Instrument:** International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR)

**Adopted:** 16 December 1966

**Adopted by:** United Nations General Assembly, Resolution 2200A (XXI)

**Entry into force:** 3 January 1976

**Legal character:** International human rights treaty

**Malaysia:** Not a State Party

The ICESCR establishes international legal standards concerning economic, social and cultural rights. It complements the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR) within the broader UN human rights treaty framework.

---

# 02 · CORE RIGHTS

The Covenant covers a broad range of economic, social and cultural rights.

Key provisions include:

### Article 1

Right of peoples to self-determination.

### Article 2

General obligations of States Parties, including progressive realization of the rights recognized in the Covenant.

### Article 6

Right to work.

### Article 7

Right to just and favourable conditions of work.

### Article 8

Right to form and join trade unions.

### Article 9

Right to social security.

### Article 10

Protection of the family, mothers and children.

### Article 11

Right to an adequate standard of living, including adequate food, clothing and housing.

### Article 12

Right to the highest attainable standard of physical and mental health.

### Article 13

Right to education.

### Article 15

Right to take part in cultural life and enjoy the benefits of scientific progress.

---

# 03 · MALAYSIA'S TREATY STATUS

Malaysia is **not a State Party to the ICESCR**.

The OHCHR treaty database does not record a signature or ratification/accession date for Malaysia under the ICESCR.

This distinction is important when analysing the relationship between international human rights standards and Malaysian domestic law.

The Covenant should therefore not be presented as a treaty binding on Malaysia in the same manner as treaties to which Malaysia is a State Party.

---

# 04 · RELEVANCE TO MALAYSIA

Although Malaysia is not a State Party to the ICESCR, the rights addressed by the Covenant remain relevant to the study of human rights and development.

Relevant areas include:

* Education
* Health
* Employment
* Social security
* Housing
* Food
* Standard of living
* Cultural participation
* Economic and social development

For example, the right to adequate food is recognised under Article 11 of the ICESCR, while Malaysia is not currently a State Party to the Covenant.

The ICESCR can therefore serve as an international reference framework when examining how economic and social rights are understood, protected and implemented.

---

# 05 · COMPARISON WITH MALAYSIAN LAW

The ICESCR provides a useful comparative framework for examining Malaysian constitutional and legislative protections.

Potential areas of comparison include:

| ICESCR                                   | Malaysian Legal Context                |
| ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Article 6 — Right to work                | Labour and employment legislation      |
| Article 9 — Social security              | Social protection frameworks           |
| Article 11 — Adequate standard of living | Housing, food and development policies |
| Article 12 — Health                      | Public health legislation and policy   |
| Article 13 — Education                   | Education legislation and policy       |
| Article 15 — Cultural participation      | Cultural and educational frameworks    |

These comparisons should not automatically be treated as evidence that Malaysia has accepted the ICESCR as domestic law.

The legal basis of each Malaysian protection must be established separately.

---

# 06 · PROGRESSIVE REALIZATION

Article 2 of the ICESCR establishes the concept of **progressive realization**.

States Parties undertake to take steps, using the maximum of their available resources, towards achieving progressively the full realization of the rights recognized in the Covenant.

This concept is particularly relevant to research concerning:

* Poverty reduction
* Public health
* Education
* Social protection
* Housing
* Economic inequality
* Sustainable development

However, because Malaysia is not a State Party to the ICESCR, the Covenant's treaty obligations do not automatically apply to Malaysia as obligations arising from the Covenant itself.

---

# 07 · TREATY BODY

The implementation of the ICESCR by States Parties is monitored by the:

**Committee on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (CESCR)**

The Committee examines State reports and develops authoritative interpretations of the Covenant through its work.

Because Malaysia is not a State Party to the ICESCR, Malaysia does not participate in the CESCR reporting process as a State Party under the Covenant.

# 08 · RESEARCH SIGNIFICANCE

The ICESCR is important to this research because it provides a framework for examining the relationship between:

**Human Rights → Development → Domestic Law → Public Policy**

It can assist in analysing whether particular economic, social and cultural rights are reflected in Malaysian constitutional provisions, legislation, institutions and public policy.

The analysis must distinguish between:

* International treaty obligations
* International human rights standards
* Malaysian constitutional rights
* Malaysian legislation
* Public policy
* Institutional practice
* Academic interpretations

# 10 · RESEARCH CAUTION

The ICESCR should not be cited as though it were a treaty obligation currently binding on Malaysia.

The research must distinguish between:

> **Malaysia's existing legal obligations**

and

> **international standards that provide a comparative or normative framework.**

This distinction is essential for accurate legal research.
