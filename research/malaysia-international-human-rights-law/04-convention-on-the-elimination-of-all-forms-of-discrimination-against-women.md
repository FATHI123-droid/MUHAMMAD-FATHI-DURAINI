# Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women

### International Human Rights Instrument 02

**Research Project:** Malaysia and International Human Rights Law

---

## SOURCE IDENTIFICATION

**Title:** Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women
**Abbreviation:** CEDAW
**Adopted:** 18 December 1979
**Entry into Force:** 3 September 1981
**Source Type:** International Human Rights Treaty
**Depositary:** Secretary-General of the United Nations

Malaysia acceded to CEDAW on **5 July 1995**. The Convention entered into force for Malaysia on **4 August 1995**.

---

# WHY THIS SOURCE MATTERS

CEDAW is a major international human rights treaty addressing discrimination against women and the promotion of equality between women and men.

For this research project, CEDAW provides an important case study for examining the relationship between:

* International human rights obligations
* Malaysian constitutional protections
* Domestic legislation
* Government policy
* International treaty monitoring
* Human rights institutions

CEDAW is particularly relevant because Malaysia is a State Party and has participated in the treaty's reporting and review process before the Committee on the Elimination of Discrimination against Women. The UN treaty-body database records Malaysia's sixth periodic report and related CEDAW documentation.

# MALAYSIA'S TREATY STATUS

Malaysia acceded to CEDAW on **5 July 1995**.

The Convention entered into force for Malaysia on **4 August 1995**.

Malaysia's participation in CEDAW must be examined together with its reservations to particular provisions.

Therefore, the research should distinguish between:

**CEDAW as an international treaty**

and

**the specific treaty position maintained by Malaysia.**

---

# RESERVATIONS

Malaysia has historically entered reservations to several CEDAW provisions.

SUHAKAM's CEDAW-related material records that Malaysia initially entered reservations concerning Articles 2(f), 5(a), 7(b), 9 and 16.

The same material records subsequent withdrawals of several reservations, including withdrawals in 1998 and 2010.

The research should therefore examine Malaysia's treaty position chronologically rather than treating the reservations as static.

### Research sequence

**Accession**

↓

**Initial Reservations**

↓

**Withdrawal / Modification of Reservations**

↓

**Current Treaty Position**

↓

**Domestic Implementation**

The exact current reservation status should be verified against the United Nations Treaty Collection before the final research paper is written.

# ARTICLE 16 — MARRIAGE AND FAMILY RELATIONS

Article 16 addresses equality in marriage and family relations.

This provision is particularly important for the Malaysian context because its implementation must be examined alongside:

* Federal constitutional provisions
* Civil legislation
* Islamic family law
* Jurisdictional arrangements
* Judicial decisions
* Malaysia's reservations

SUHAKAM's CEDAW material records that Malaysia has maintained reservations to certain provisions of Article 16.

The research should present the legal position accurately without treating international treaty standards, domestic law and religious legal frameworks as interchangeable categories.

# CEDAW COMMITTEE AND TREATY MONITORING

The Committee on the Elimination of Discrimination against Women monitors implementation of CEDAW by States Parties.

Malaysia has submitted periodic reports to the Committee.

The UN Treaty Body Database records Malaysia's **sixth periodic report, CEDAW/C/MYS/6**, submitted in 2022, together with subsequent treaty-body documentation.

These materials provide evidence for examining:

* Malaysia's own description of implementation
* Questions raised by the Committee
* Government responses
* Committee observations
* Follow-up processes
* Areas requiring further legal or policy analysis

# SUHAKAM AND CEDAW

SUHAKAM has participated in the CEDAW process and has produced material concerning Malaysia's implementation of the Convention.

A SUHAKAM report submitted to the CEDAW Committee describes the Commission's observations concerning Government responses and implementation of CEDAW recommendations, as well as its monitoring of laws and policies affecting women's rights in Malaysia.
