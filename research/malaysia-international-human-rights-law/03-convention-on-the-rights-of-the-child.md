# WHY THIS SOURCE MATTERS

The CRC is a major international human rights treaty concerning the rights of children.

For this research project, the Convention provides an important case study for examining the relationship between:

* International treaty obligations
* Malaysian domestic law
* Constitutional protections
* National policies
* Institutional implementation
* SUHAKAM's human rights mandate

The CRC is particularly useful because Malaysia is a State Party, while its participation has included reservations to certain provisions. This allows the research to examine how international human rights obligations interact with Malaysia's domestic legal framework.

# MALAYSIA'S TREATY STATUS

Malaysia acceded to the CRC on **17 February 1995**.

The Convention entered into force for Malaysia on **19 March 1995**.

Malaysia initially entered reservations concerning several provisions.

The United Nations Treaty Collection currently records reservations concerning:

* Article 2
* Article 7
* Article 14
* Article 28 paragraph 1(a)
* Article 37

The reservation provides that these provisions are applicable only insofar as they conform with the Constitution, national laws and national policies of Malaysia.

---

# RESERVATIONS AND WITHDRAWALS

Malaysia's CRC treaty position has changed over time.

According to United Nations treaty records, Malaysia partially withdrew reservations in 1999 and again in 2010.

In 2010, Malaysia withdrew reservations concerning Articles 1, 13 and 15 and modified its position concerning Article 28(1)(a). The remaining reservations were subsequently recorded in relation to Articles 2, 7, 14, 28(1)(a) and 37.

This history is important because treaty participation should not be analysed simply as:

**Party / Not Party**

A more accurate analysis should examine:

**Accession → Reservations → Withdrawals → Declarations → Domestic implementation**

# TREATY-BODY DIMENSION

The Committee on the Rights of the Child monitors implementation of the Convention by States Parties.

For Malaysia, the Committee's documents provide material for examining:

* State reporting
* Committee observations
* Recommendations
* Government responses
* Treaty implementation
* Reservations
* Legislative and institutional developments

Malaysia's replies to the Committee's list of issues received in January 2026 state that the Government continues to review its remaining reservations and has established an inter-ministerial committee to periodically assess treaty reservations.

This material will be examined separately from the treaty text itself.
